# Requirements

## Interview

- Detect, never assume, the state the work arrives in and who answers product questions. A raw request (from a user, customer support or the owner) is triaged and its problem restated; a written ticket (from a product owner or business analyst) is checked against its own intent and its gaps sent back to its author, not re-elicited; with no ticket, intent is drawn out by interview and written up as one. Engineering and agents do not make product decisions: route each open question, concretely and with screenshots where useful, to whoever owns them, a product team or one person working with agents.
- Run a requirements interview as a domain-agnostic discovery aid. Help a person without a clear vision find their intent without letting them lean on the agent too much. Move between the macro view and drill-down sub-lanes and back to the main thread, and tell them "I don't know" is a valid answer.
- End a requirements discussion with no open assumptions or ambiguities, except those explicitly deferred to a named later phase. Close with a recap of every question and answer and a check that every decision branch was walked.
- Send open requirement questions to the person who can answer them through an easy shared link, not a static document.

## Interview and plan

- Settle the problem, goal and definition of done before planning or building. Draw out intent through interviews without prescribing solutions.
- Map a large foggy effort early, with a spec as the destination. A first session charts the map, not every answer. Make rounds efficient rather than short, and never overturn settled answers.

## Interview and build

- When refining a work item, restate its existing intent as testable acceptance criteria grounded in its own words or recorded decisions, and never invent scope. Hold scope while still accounting for every data piece, dependency and affected system.
- Keep a spec to the product perspective and acceptance criteria, with no implementation details. Treat specs as temporary artifacts that stay out of source control long term.
